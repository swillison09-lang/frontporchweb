A letter to you

Dear friend,

We do not know your name yet, or your story, but we want you to know: we think you are brave. Considering an adoption plan for your child is one of the most loving, selfless things a person can do, and we do not take lightly that you are even reading these words.

If you choose us, we promise your child will grow up knowing they were wanted by everyone in their story, including you. They will have pancakes on Sundays, a dog who follows them everywhere, a yard to run in, and two parents who will show up, every single day, with everything we have.

And we promise to honor you. However much or little contact feels right to you, we will respect it. Your courage will always be part of our family's story, told with love and gratitude.

There is no pressure and no rush. Take your time. Whatever you decide, we are holding hope for you and your baby.

With love and hope,
Marcus & Emily

Our story

Eight years, one big dream.

We met in 2016 at a friend's wedding. Marcus was playing guitar in the band, and Emily, so the story goes, caught the bouquet that same night.

We like to think someone was trying to tell us something. We were married two years later under a big oak tree, surrounded by the people we love most.

Ever since, we have been building a quiet, joyful life together: Sunday pancakes, long walks with the dog, music in the kitchen, and dreaming of the day we get to share it with a child of our own through adoption.

Husband & dad-to-be

Marcus

Marcus is a high-school band director who has never met a song he could not hum. He is patient, a little goofy, and the first to volunteer for the messy job. Weekends usually find him hiking a trail, coaching Little League, or fixing something in the garage with the radio on.

Our home & community

A cozy house on a quiet street.

Home is a three-bedroom house in Carmel, Indiana, with a big backyard, a front-porch swing, and a kitchen that always smells like something.

We are a short walk from a leafy park, a library, and some of the best-rated schools in the state. Our neighborhood is full of families, sidewalk chalk, and friendly faces.

Down the hall

There is a room that is ready.

Emily painted it a soft green last spring. The crib was her grandmother's, stripped back and repainted over a long weekend with the windows open.

We are not in a hurry to fill it. We just wanted you to see that there is a place here, and it has been waiting a while.
